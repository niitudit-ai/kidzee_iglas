About the Advocate

Krishna Kant Chaturvedy, Advocate

Practising at the District & Sessions Court, Aligarh, with a particular focus on family and criminal matters. The working belief is simple: before a case can be won, the client must actually understand what is happening in it.

Most people approach an advocate at a difficult moment — a notice has arrived, an FIR has been registered, or a family disagreement has reached court. The first task at that point is to translate the matter out of legal language: what your options are, what outcome is realistically possible, and what it will take in time and cost.

Every matter in this chamber is handled personally. The advocate you speak to is the advocate who appears for you at the hearing.

  • Courts: District & Sessions Court, Aligarh — including the Family Court, CJM/ACJM courts and tribunals.
  • Membership: Advocates Bar Association (A.B.A.), Aligarh — Chamber No. 155.
  • Core focus: family and matrimonial disputes, and criminal defence and bail.
  • Confidentiality: everything you share is treated as privileged and kept strictly confidential.
How It Works

From the first call to the courtroom, in four clear steps

No uncertainty and no hidden costs. At every stage you know what comes next.

01

Tell us the matter

Explain briefly on a call or over WhatsApp. If you have a notice, FIR or court paper, send a photo of it.

02

Assessment

After reviewing the papers and facts, you are told where the law supports you, where the risk lies, and what is realistically achievable.

03

Strategy & fees

The plan of action and the fee structure are set out in writing — which application, in which court, and the likely timeline.

04

Representation

Drafting, filing and arguing in court. You are updated directly after each hearing, without having to chase for information.

The Approach

How this chamber works

Direct access

Your matter is not passed to a clerk or a junior. The person you speak to is the person who appears in court.

Plain language

No hiding behind sections and jargon. Hindi or English, whichever you are comfortable in.

Transparent fees

The fee is agreed in writing at the outset. No unexpected demands later on.

An honest view

If a case is weak, or a settlement serves you better, you will be told so plainly rather than encouraged into litigation.

Current law

Matters are argued under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita and Sakshya Adhiniyam in force since July 2024.

Urgent availability

In arrest, bail and stay matters, timing decides the outcome. Such matters are taken up on priority.
