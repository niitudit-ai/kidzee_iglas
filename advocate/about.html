About the Advocate

Krishna Kant Chaturvedy practises at the District & Sessions Court, Aligarh, with a focus on family and criminal matters.

Profile

Krishna Kant Chaturvedy, Advocate

A large part of an advocate's work is not argument but explanation — helping the client understand what is actually happening. In this chamber that is treated as the first task.

People generally come to an advocate at a hard point in their lives. A marriage may be breaking down, an FIR may have been registered against a family member, or a land dispute that has simmered for years may finally have reached court. At that moment the first thing that needs to be cleared is confusion and fear, and that is only possible through straight, plain conversation.

Three things stay constant in the way this chamber works: an honest assessment of the matter, complete transparency about fees, and personal appearance by the advocate himself. Where a settlement would genuinely serve the client better, that is said plainly rather than stretching litigation out.

  • Practice: District & Sessions Court, Aligarh — including the Family Court, CJM/ACJM courts, revenue courts and tribunals.
  • Bar membership: Advocates Bar Association (A.B.A.), Aligarh — Chamber No. 155.
  • Areas of focus: family and matrimonial disputes and criminal defence and bail; also civil, property, revenue, cheque bounce, accident claim and consumer matters.
  • Languages: Hindi and English — consultations in whichever you are comfortable with.
  • Confidentiality: everything shared in a consultation is treated as privileged and confidential.
What to Expect

What happens in the first meeting

The purpose of the first meeting is not to start a case, but to decide whether there should be one.

You are heard fully

No rushing. The whole sequence of events is heard first, because the legally decisive fact is often buried in what the client considers unimportant.

Papers are examined

Whatever is available — notice, FIR, summons, deed, cheque or bank statement — is examined, and you are told which further documents will be needed.

A realistic opinion

You are told where the law supports you, where the weakness lies, and what outcome is realistically possible. No inflated assurances.

Options compared

Litigation, settlement, mediation or simply waiting — each option is compared on time, cost and risk.

Fees in writing

The fee structure is put in writing before the matter is accepted, so nothing is left ambiguous.

Deadlines flagged

If a statutory deadline for bail, appeal or a reply to notice is close, that is pointed out first — missing it closes off options.
